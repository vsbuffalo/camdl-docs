What this means for the chapter

Two distinct identifiability problems, both load-bearing. The first (seed, τ) ridge is what makes our free-everything fits land late: the data prefer the high-seed corner. The second (ρ, τ) ridge is what makes our literature-ρ fits land at the "wrong" τ if the true ρ differs from the assumed one: a wrong anchor passes the error straight into τ. Neither ridge is fixable from the case-data side — they're both structural non-identifiabilities. The fix is external information that breaks them: genomic monophyly pins seed (top-row profiles all assumed seed=1 and recovered truth EXACTLY when the truth also had seed=1, i.e., the τ=−20 row); deaths or serology pin ρ (bottom-row profiles all assumed ρ=0.10 and would recover truth exactly when truth also had ρ=0.10, i.e., the τ=−21 row).

The chapter's three-act structure is now sharper: (1) cases-only free fit lands at late τ — demonstrate via real WA data; (2) demonstrate non-identifiability via this synthetic experiment (both ridges); (3) show that two independent external constraints (monophyly + literature ρ from sero/deaths) jointly identify τ — this is what the profile on real WA data does, and the answer lands at the genomic window. Same lesson as Raue 2009 but with the modern epi-vs-genomics + epi-vs-sero dual-stream framing.

13. The "seed pulse" is a misnomer — it's actually a logistic ONSET of SUSTAINED imports

Calling out an error in my framing and (likely) the chapter prose. The transition seed : --> I @ λ/(1+exp(-(t-τ)/w)) is a rate, not a pulse. After the logistic switch opens, the import rate stays at λ forever — so cumulative expected imports grow linearly at λ per day with no upper bound. At literature truth (λ=2, τ=−12, w=3): 4.2 imports by 12 Feb, 24 imports by 24 Feb, 66 imports by 16 Mar. This is NOT "one founding event" — it's a sustained external-introduction process. τ is the midpoint of the import-rate onset, not the time of a single injection.

Why this matters for the bias we've been seeing. What the case data actually identify is total imports up to time t (in the pre-saturation regime, the cumulative epidemic is proportional to ∫import rate · accumulated growth). Two ways the model can reduce total imports while still fitting a small observed curve: (a) reduce λ uniformly, or (b) push τ later so the integral starts later. The posterior takes BOTH — that's exactly the (late τ, low λ) corner-picking on the (τ, λ) ridge in panel 12. The bias is partly a property of using a sustained-import model when the data want fewer net imports.

Remediation options:

  • Bell-shaped pulse (recommended for "one-shot" semantics): seed : --> I @ λ·exp(−((t−τ)/w)²) (Gaussian) or λ·sech²((t−τ)/w). Finite integral = λ·w·√π (Gaussian); after τ+few·w the rate returns to 0. λ now has units of "peak imports/day", and total imports = O(λ·w) is finite. Genuinely a pulse.
  • Truncated logistic: ramp up at τ, ramp down at τ+Δ. Two-parameter onset/offset.
  • Reinterpret the existing model: drop the "pulse" language entirely. τ is "when sustained external transmission pressure turned on" — which is actually a defensible thing to estimate for a place receiving ongoing imports (most of Jan–Mar WA). This reframing is consistent with the MMWR retrospective surveillance (zero through Feb 20 ≈ sustained import rate ≈ 0 through Feb 20).
  • Two-pulse, bell-shaped K=2: combines this fix with the multi-import model comparison we discussed.

Most useful experiment to run next: swap the seed rate to a Gaussian pulse on synthetic data simulated also from a Gaussian pulse, and refit. Does the bias on (τ, λ) go away? If yes, the bias on the current model is partly the integral-runaway. If the bias persists, it's purely the (τ, λ) ridge mechanism and the sustained vs. pulse distinction is incidental.

12. λ-specific diagnostics — the (τ, λ) ridge is the dominant degree of freedom

λ is the asymptotic seed-pulse importation rate (individuals/day, once the logistic switch fully opens). Truth = 2.0; posterior median = 0.92 (biased ~2.2× low); 95% CI = [0.07, 5.54] — almost 2 decades wide, essentially unidentified. Prior is Exp(rate=0.5) with mean = 2 (perfectly calibrated to truth) yet the posterior pulls mass away from truth toward zero — the data is contributing to the bias, not just the prior.

Why: the model trajectory at any time depends primarily on λ·exp(r(t−τ)), so the data identify the linear combination ln λ = r·τ + const tightly but not λ or τ individually. The chapter's wa-posterior-ridge cell already establishes this for the real-data fit. On the linear λ axis the ridge looks like a modest +0.57 correlation; on the log axis it sharpens to +0.76. Truth lies exactly ON the analytical gold ridge line (chosen to pass through it), and the posterior density is concentrated along the ridge but biased toward the late-τ / low-λ corner. λ inherits all of τ's looseness, exponentially amplified by the growth rate r ≈ 0.23: an 8-day τ overestimate predicts a ~6× λ underestimate — and we observe ~2× (modulated by the prior).

On both ridges the red truth point sits in the lower-tail / corner of the density: the posterior covers truth at 90% but its mass concentrates at the ridge corner away from truth. The β–γ panel is the clearest "two parameters, one piece of information" picture in the chapter — the density is a tight ribbon along a constant-r line.

11b. ρ FIXED at truth (0.10)

Removing ρ as a free parameter does not break the β–γ ridge (corr now +0.94 — even tighter without ρ to absorb some of the joint uncertainty). A new τ–β corr +0.58 appears: with ρ pinned, the constraint partly migrates into τ and partly into β (we already saw this on real WA data — fixing ρ at literature raises R₀ to ~2.9). Truth points (red stars) sit inside the densities but again toward the corners, not at the modes — confirming that the residual τ bias is structural ridge-corner-picking, not a methods bug.

10. Synth recovery with ρ pinned at truth — τ STILL biased ~6 d late

Same synthetic data as panel 9, but ρ now FIXED at truth (0.10) in the fit. τ posterior median shifts from −4.1 → −5.6 (only ~1.5 d closer to truth). P(τ inside Bedford genomic window [−19, −5]) rises 0.235 → 0.415, but the bulk of the posterior is still later than the genomic window. Truth (τ=−12) sits at the 30% quantile. Even with PERFECT ρ on data we know came from the literature parameters, the case-count likelihood cannot recover the early-τ truth. The reported-case data simply do not carry information about how far back the seeding actually went — only the growth rate (recovered tightly at 0.239 ± 0.03 vs truth 0.230) and the late-stage trajectory are well-identified. This is a fundamental property of fitting case data to a single-pulse SIR.

9. Synthetic recovery — pipeline validates (every truth in 90% CI), but τ/ρ posterior biased to the ridge corner

PMMH on synthetic data generated at literature parameters (constant-ρ model, same priors as real-WA fit, ρ free). All 6 truth parameters lie inside the 90% CI — the inference pipeline is sound on this problem. Critically, the bias pattern on synth data matches the real-data finding: τ posterior median is −4 (truth −12), ρ posterior peaks near 0.20 (truth 0.10). The data + this model + vague priors generically drift to the high-ρ / late-τ ridge corner even when the truth is at low-ρ / early-τ. This is a property of the (ρ, τ) identifiability ridge, not a bug. Two implications: (1) real-data divergence from literature is the same ridge behavior, not a methods error; (2) decision-making τ estimates on early-outbreak data REQUIRE an external constraint on ρ (or τ via genomics / surveillance) — vague-prior point estimates will systematically land on the wrong corner.

7. ρ fixed at literature (0.10) vs ρ free — τ shifts only 4 d earlier; β rises to absorb the constraint

Same model + sampler as the constant-ρ free fit, but ρ pinned at 0.10 (central published US/WA early-2020 ascertainment, see writeup). τ posterior median moves from 27 Feb → 23 Feb (~4 d earlier), 95% CI [03 Feb, 04 Mar] now covers the genomic window. P(τ inside Bedford genomic MRCA window [5 Feb – 19 Feb]) rises from 0.10 (free) to 0.25 (lit-fixed) — but most of the posterior is still later than the genomic window. Right panel: β rises from 0.37 → 0.40 (R0 2.5 → 2.9) to absorb the constraint — fixing ρ at low literature doesn't only buy "earlier τ", it also buys "higher R0". Penalty for accepting ρ = 0.10: MAP loglik drops from −78.54 to −81.66, i.e. Δ = 3.12 nats = 13.6 dB — strong evidence against ρ = 0.10 from this data + model alone.

6. Free constant-ρ fit (no ramp at all) vs literature

PMMH on the constant-ρ structural-comparison model. Same priors, same data, same sampler — the only change vs the ramp model is dropping rho(t) for a single scalar ρ. Left: posterior on ρ for both models, with the published early-2020 US/WA case-ascertainment band (4–17%) shaded green. The free ρ posterior median is 0.60, 95% CI [0.21, 0.89] — order-of-magnitude above the literature band, with essentially zero mass at ρ ≤ 0.17. Right: τ posterior for both models. With ρ free, both models put τ near or after 24 Feb (constant-ρ median 27 Feb, ramp median 24 Feb), later than the genomic MRCA window (~5 Feb – 19 Feb). The genomic-literature joint constraint (low ρ × early τ) is not where the data alone want to live; it sits in the lower tail of both posteriors. Compare ELPDs: the constant-ρ model actually fits slightly better than the ramp (Δelpd +0.79, +3.4 dB but indeterminate, se 1.26) — the ramp isn't doing identifiable work on this data.
